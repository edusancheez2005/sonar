## D fanned deck (1440 and 390)

- Strength: the info sheet (product, amount, place, 3 metric tiles, Asset and Capital rows, 02 / 05 counter) is the clearest terms layout of all concepts; it animates in with a short stagger.
- Strength: the fanned cards with the gold ring on the active card and dimming of the others look premium and clearly show which deal is open (D-tr-700-1440.png, D-tr-1500-1440.png).
- Problem: pin:true holds the page for 1,040 px (pin-spacer 1,820 px at 1440; section 2,436 px tall); this is scroll-jacking under the CONTEXT rules and makes the section slow to pass for anyone not interested.
- Problem: only one deal is legible at a time; the other cards show an amount and a city only, so the breadth of the record is weaker than F's list.
- Problem: selection is driven by scroll progress, so keyboard users cannot step through deals except by scrolling; clicking a card scrolls the page.
- Mobile (<700 px): falls back to a horizontal snap scroller of cards with the sheet below (D-tr-450-390.png, identical to E); no hint or arrows on D at this width.
- 0 console errors, 0 overflow at both widths.

## E swipe deck (1440 and 390)

- Strength: the page scroll is never held; the deck moves only sideways by drag, swipe, arrows, dots or arrow keys; the wheel group has tabindex, role=group and aria-roledescription=carousel.
- Strength: clear affordance, prev and next arrows, dots with aria-current and a one-time hint 'Swipe, drag or use the arrows' that fades after first use (E-tr-300-1440.png).
- Problem: cards are 200 x 264 px in a 540 to 640 px stage, so a lot of empty cream; the fifth card is cut off by the edge mask.
- Problem: horizontal wheel and shift plus wheel over the deck are preventDefaulted, which surprises trackpad users scrolling the page.
- Problem: like D, one deal legible at a time; the deal story is the least clear of the 3 files.
- Mobile: same horizontal snap scroller plus sheet as D; swipe works (sheet changes to Sheffield, 02 after a swipe).
- 0 console errors, 0 overflow at both widths.

## 4. Feed assessment

**Change semantics vs the approved panel.** The feed's change is the size of the last change (BoE: the 18 Dec 2025 cut; Fed: the 16/17 Sep 2026 rise) and, for Euribor, the move on the previous fixing. The approved odometer panel labels the latest decision (Held, 17 Sep 2026 decision) and the latest fixing (Down 2.0 bps, 27 Oct 2026 fixing). These are different facts. The feed has no field that says when the latest decision was or what it was, so a panel driven by today's feed could truthfully show only Cut 25 bps, 18 Dec 2025 and Raised 25 bps, 17 Sep 2026, and for Euribor it would have to infer the previous fixing's date because changed_on equals as_of.

**Fields the feed would need** (per rate, additive, keep the current fields for the live footer script):
- `as_of`: date of the data point (BoE: date of the latest Bank Rate row, not today); `published_at` for Euribor.
- `previous_value`, `previous_as_of`, `change_bps` (integer basis points, 25, -25, 2.5 for Euribor can stay decimal).
- `last_change_on`, `last_change_bps` (today's changed_on and change).
- `last_decision_date`, `last_decision_outcome` (held | cut | raised), `next_decision_date`.
- `move_display` (Held, Cut 25 bps, Raised 25 bps, Down 2.0 bps, Up 2.5 bps, Unchanged) and `move_note` (17 Sep 2026 decision, 27 Oct 2026 fixing) so the website prints strings and never derives copy.
- `attribution` string per rate; top-level `schema_version`, `checked` (already present) and `updated_display` renamed in meaning to checked.

Example (BoE, as it should read on 31 Oct 2026): value 3.75, display 3.75%, as_of 2026-10-30, previous_value 4.00, last_change_on 2025-12-18, last_change_bps -25, last_decision_date 2026-09-17, last_decision_outcome held, next_decision_date 2026-11-05, move_display Held, move_note 17 Sep 2026 decision, attribution Source: Bank of England, Open Government Licence v3.0. After 5 Nov 2026 the panel would read Held, 5 Nov 2026 decision (or Raised 25 bps, 5 Nov 2026 decision) with no site change.

**How to get decision dates without a paid source.** Keep a `calendar.json` in the repo with the published MPC dates (bankofengland.co.uk, Monetary Policy Committee dates) and FOMC dates (federalreserve.gov, FOMC meeting calendars) for the current and following year; the script sets last_decision_date to the most recent date not after today, outcome held if last_change_on is earlier than that date, else cut or raised by sign. Add a yearly GitHub issue reminder to extend the calendar. For Euribor, previous_as_of is simply the prior row of the table.

**Robustness.** Validate FRED's CSV header, numeric ranges (0 to 25) and ISO dates and raise on failure so the existing stale fallback is used (would have prevented the 400% incident). The site script should also refuse to print a value outside 0 to 25 and should honour `stale`.

**Licensing summary.**
- EMMI (Euribor administrator): delayed rates viewable free after registration with a 24-hour delay and a rolling 25-publication-day window, solely for non-commercial purposes; subscription mandatory for any commercial use (https://www.emmi-benchmarks.eu/benchmarks/euribor/rate/, https://www.emmi-benchmarks.eu/faq/subscriptions/). Delayed Data licence for website publication, 1,060 euro per hosted website per year in the 2021-2022 schedule (https://www.emmi-benchmarks.eu/globalassets/documents/pdf/communication/news/d0344a-2021-2022-fee-schedule.pdf); 2024-2025 schedule requires a licence to distribute delayed data (https://www.emmi-benchmarks.eu/globalassets/documents/pdf/subscriptions/d0196b-2024-2025-fee-schedule.pdf); 2026-2027 schedule not readable from this session (https://www.emmi-benchmarks.eu/globalassets/documents/pdf/subscriptions/d0167a-2026---2027-fee-schedule.pdf). euribor-rates.eu (Triami Media B.V.) credits EMMI and reserves all rights (https://www.euribor-rates.eu/disclaimer.asp); its sister site global-rates.com requires written permission for reproduction beyond personal use (https://www.global-rates.com/en/disclaimer/). The feed's README already records that EMMI says redisplay on a commercial site needs a licence.
