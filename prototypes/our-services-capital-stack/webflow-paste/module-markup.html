Where Roscap works in the stack

  1. 1 Common Equity

    Joint venture equity and co-investment

    The top of the stack: the position that takes the first loss and carries the upside. Roscap raises joint venture equity and arranges direct co-investment for developers, sponsors and family offices, in shared-risk structures with aligned upside and objectives that stay consistent from entry to exit.

    Equity and co-investment

  2. 2 Preferred Equity

    Preferred equity

    Sits behind the debt and ahead of the common equity. Roscap structures fixed-return positions behind senior debt with potential profit participation, often used to reduce the sponsor’s own equity requirement while the senior facility stays conventional.

    Equity and co-investment

  3. 3 Mezzanine Debt

    Mezzanine finance

    The top of the debt. Roscap arranges mezzanine finance as subordinated capital to enhance leverage or bridge equity requirements, negotiated alongside the senior facility so that the intercreditor terms work for both lenders and the sponsor.

    Real estate debt advisory

  4. 4 Senior Debt

    Development finance, bridging finance, and commercial and BTL mortgages

    The foundation of the stack, secured by a first charge over the asset. This is where most of Roscap’s debt advisory sits: development finance for ground-up, conversion or renovation projects, bridging finance for acquisitions, refinancing or equity release, and income-based term lending for stabilised assets. Stretch-senior facilities sit here too, taking the senior position further up the stack.

    Real estate debt advisory
